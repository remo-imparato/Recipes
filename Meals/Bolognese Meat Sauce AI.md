# **Bolognese Ragù (Refined, Traditional Technique)**

## **Ingredients**
- 1 Tbsp olive oil  
- 3 Tbsp unsalted butter  
- 1 large yellow onion, finely diced  
- 2 celery stalks, finely diced  
- 2 carrots, finely diced  
- 1 lb ground beef chuck (80/20), preferably from the neck  
  *Optional but classic:* add 3 oz minced pancetta or replace 25–30% of the beef with ground pork  
- Salt  
- 1 cup whole milk  
- ½ tsp ground nutmeg  
- 1 cup dry white wine (or dry red for deeper flavor)  
- 1 can (14 oz) whole Italian plum tomatoes, crushed by hand  
  *(Or use 28 oz but drain half the liquid)*  
- Freshly ground black pepper  
- Beef or chicken broth, as needed (1–2 cups)

---

# **Instructions**

### **1. Build the soffritto**
Heat the olive oil and butter in a heavy pot over medium‑low heat. Add the onion, celery, and carrot with a pinch of salt. Cook slowly for **10–15 minutes**, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are soft, sweet, and translucent. They should melt, not brown.

### **2. Add and brown the meat**
Add the ground beef (and pancetta, if using). Break it up gently with a wooden spoon. Season with salt immediately to draw out moisture.  
Cook over medium heat until the meat’s liquid evaporates and it begins to **lightly brown**. This may take **10–15 minutes**.

### **3. Milk stage**
Pour in the milk and add the nutmeg. Simmer gently until the milk has fully reduced and only the fat remains — **20–30 minutes**.  
This step protects the meat from acidity and creates the signature sweetness.

### **4. Wine reduction**
Add the wine. Let it simmer until it has completely evaporated — **10–15 minutes**.  
No wine aroma should remain.

### **5. Add tomatoes**
Stir in the crushed tomatoes. When the mixture returns to a simmer, reduce the heat to the gentlest bubble you can maintain.

### **6. Long, slow cooking**
Cook **uncovered** for **3–6 hours**, stirring occasionally.  
Add small ladlefuls of broth as needed to keep the sauce moist but thick.  
The ragù should become dense, glossy, and brick‑orange rather than red.

### **7. Final adjustments**
Taste and adjust:
- If too tight → add a splash of broth  
- If too loose → simmer uncovered a bit longer  
- If it needs richness → swirl in 1 Tbsp butter  

Finish with black pepper.

---

# **Serving**
Serve with fresh tagliatelle, pappardelle, or use as the base for lasagne alla Bolognese.  
Top with freshly grated Parmigiano‑Reggiano.
