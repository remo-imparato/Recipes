# Weed Butter

## Ingredients

### Decarboxylation

- 1/2 ounce cannabis flower

### Stovetop infusion

- 1 1/2 cups water
- 8 ounces (2 sticks) clarified butter, melted butter, or oil
- 1/2 ounce decarboxylated cannabis

## Equipment

- Hand grinder or scissors
- Glass baking dish or sheet pan
- Medium saucepan with lid
- Wooden spoon
- Thermometer (optional)
- Cheesecloth or metal strainer (optional)
- Container for storage

## Directions

### 1. Decarboxylate the cannabis

1. Preheat the oven to 220°F.
2. Gently break apart the cannabis flower with a grinder, scissors, or your hands. Keep it coarse enough that it will not pass through the strainer.
3. Spread the cannabis evenly in a glass baking dish or on a sheet pan. Bake on the center rack:
   - About 20 minutes for older or lower-quality cannabis
   - About 45 minutes for cured, high-grade cannabis
   - 1 hour or longer for recently harvested, moist cannabis
4. Check frequently and gently stir every 10 minutes to prevent burning. The source says the color will change from bright green to deep brownish green when decarboxylated.

### 2. Infuse the butter on the stove top

1. Add the water and butter to a medium saucepan over very low heat.
2. When the butter has melted, add the decarboxylated cannabis. Stir well and cover the saucepan.
3. Let the mixture simmer gently for 4 hours, stirring every 30 minutes. If using a thermometer, keep the temperature below 180°F.
4. Strain the mixture through cheesecloth or a metal strainer into a container.
5. Let the butter cool to room temperature. Use immediately, or store it in a well-sealed container in the refrigerator or freezer. The source says it can be stored for up to 6 months.

## Dosage note

The source estimates 292 mg THC in the full batch when using 20% THC cannabis, and estimates about 12 mg per cookie when divided among 24 cookies. It also states 30 mg per teaspoon, but that does not match the batch estimate: 2 sticks of butter contain 96 teaspoons, so 292 mg divided evenly would be about 3 mg per teaspoon. Actual potency and infusion yield vary; do not rely on these estimates as a precise dose.
